## 1. Home

### Living Daya: Compassion for All Creation

> ਦਇਆ ਕਪਾਹ ਸੰਤੋਖੁ ਸੂਤੁ ਜਤੁ ਗੰਢੀ ਸਤੁ ਵਟੁ ॥
> *"Make compassion the cotton, contentment the thread, self-restraint the knot, and truth the twist."*
> — Guru Nanak Dev Ji, Asa Ki Vaar, SGGS Ang 471

Living Daya is a resource for Sikhs and friends of the Sikh tradition. It asks how
our food choices today can reflect the Gurus' teachings of **Daya** (compassion),
**Sarbat da Bhala** (the well-being of all), and living in harmony with **Kudrat**
(the Creator's creation).

Food production has changed dramatically in a single lifetime. Industrial dairy
and meat production now involve suffering, slaughter and environmental harm on a
scale earlier generations never saw. This site explains what happens in those
systems and offers practical, nourishing plant-based alternatives for those who
choose them.

### Our approach
Gurbani does not set out a diet, and Sikhs hold sincere, differing views on food.
We don't claim scriptural rulings, and we don't judge anyone's path. We share
Gurbani on compassion, facts about modern food production, and practical guidance,
so each reader can reflect for themselves. Nothing here is meant to change
Panthic Maryada.

## 2. Gurbani on Daya

In Gurbani, compassion is woven into spiritual life and daily conduct. It is an
active virtue, not a passing feeling.

### The sacred thread of the soul
> ਦਇਆ ਕਪਾਹ ਸੰਤੋਖੁ ਸੂਤੁ ਜਤੁ ਗੰਢੀ ਸਤੁ ਵਟੁ ॥
> ਏਹੁ ਜਨੇਊ ਜੀਅ ਕਾ ਹਈ ਤ ਪਾਡੇ ਘਤੁ ॥
> ਨਾ ਏਹੁ ਤੁਟੈ ਨਾ ਮਲੁ ਲਗੈ ਨਾ ਏਹੁ ਜਲੈ ਨ ਜਾਇ ॥
> ਧੰਨੁ ਸੁ ਮਾਣਸ ਨਾਨਕਾ ਜੋ ਗਲਿ ਚਲੇ ਪਾਇ ॥
>
> *"Make compassion the cotton, contentment the thread, self-restraint the knot,
> and truth the twist. This is the sacred thread of the soul — O Pandit, if you
> have it, then put it on me. It does not break, it is not soiled, it is not
> burnt, nor is it lost. Blessed are those, O Nanak, who go about wearing it
> around their necks."*
> — Guru Nanak Dev Ji, Asa Ki Vaar (Salok M1), SGGS Ang 471

### One Giver of all beings
> ਸਭਨਾ ਜੀਆ ਕਾ ਇਕੁ ਦਾਤਾ ਸੋ ਮੈ ਵਿਸਰਿ ਨ ਜਾਈ ॥
> *"There is One Giver of all living beings; may I never forget Him."*
> — Guru Nanak Dev Ji, Japji Sahib, SGGS Ang 2

### The One Lord within all
> ਜਉ ਸਭ ਮਹਿ ਏਕੁ ਖੁਦਾਇ ਕਹਤ ਹਉ ਤਉ ਕਿਉ ਮੁਰਗੀ ਮਾਰੈ ॥੧॥
> *"If you say that the One God is within all, then why do you kill the hen?"*
> — Bhagat Kabir Ji, Raag Prabhati, SGGS Ang 1350

*Context:* Bhagat Kabir Ji addresses a Mullah here, and questions whether killing
in God's name fits the belief that God lives in every being.

### Compassion among the highest merits
> ਅਠਸਠਿ ਤੀਰਥ ਸਗਲ ਪੁੰਨ ਜੀਅ ਦਇਆ ਪਰਵਾਨੁ ॥
> *"[Such a one obtains the merit of] the sixty-eight places of pilgrimage, all
> virtuous deeds, and compassion for living beings — and is accepted."*
> — Guru Arjan Dev Ji, Barah Maha (Maagh), SGGS Ang 136

*Context:* In the Maagh stanza, these merits belong to the one who, by God's grace,
meditates on Naam in the company of the holy. Compassion for beings is named
among the highest of them.

### Reflection
Gurbani says again and again that the One Light lives in all beings. Many Sikhs
find that seeing Ik Oankar in every creature leads naturally to wanting to spare
them unnecessary suffering.

## 3. Dairy and Meat: Connected Industries

Many Sikhs keep a lacto-vegetarian diet from a sincere wish not to harm animals.
But modern commercial dairy is economically tied to the slaughter of cattle.

### How commercial dairy works
- **Repeated pregnancy:** A cow gives milk only after giving birth, so dairy cows
  are made pregnant about once a year, usually by artificial insemination.
- **Separation of mother and calf:** On most commercial farms in the US, UK and
  Europe, calves are taken from their mothers within 1–2 days of birth so the milk
  can be sold.
- **Male calves:** Male calves cannot produce milk.
  - *In Western countries* they are usually killed shortly after birth or raised
    for veal or beef.
  - *In India, including Punjab,* where cow slaughter is largely banned, male
    calves are often neglected, abandoned, or sold into informal trade and
    transported for slaughter across state or national borders. 🔎 add a source
- **Shortened lives:** Cows can live around 20 years. In commercial dairy they are
  usually culled at about 4–6 years, once milk yields drop.

> **Key insight:** Buying commercial dairy financially supports a system that
> depends on the deaths of calves and cows. Plant-based alternatives let our
> intention of compassion and our daily actions line up.

## 4. Sarbat da Bhala: Caring for Creation

Sikhs pray daily for *Sarbat da Bhala*, the well-being of all. Animal agriculture
is one of the largest human impacts on the planet:

- **Greenhouse gases:** Livestock accounts for about **12–14.5%** of human-caused
  greenhouse gas emissions (FAO 2013: 14.5%; FAO 2023 updated estimate: ~12%).
- **Land:** Livestock and its feed use about **83% of the world's farmland** but
  provide only **18% of our calories** and **37% of our protein** (Poore & Nemecek,
  *Science*, 2018).
- **Fresh water:** Producing 1 litre of dairy milk withdraws about **628 L** of
  fresh water, against ~**28 L** for soy milk and ~**48 L** for oat milk
  (almond: ~**371 L**) (Poore & Nemecek 2018, via Our World in Data). 🔎

Plant-based eating uses less land, water and climate impact, which leaves more room
for forests, wildlife and future generations.

## 5. Health & Nutrition

Major dietetic bodies state that well-planned vegan diets are healthful and
nutritionally adequate for all stages of life, including for athletes (Academy of
Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016).

### Essential: Vitamin B12
Vegans need a **reliable source of vitamin B12**, either a supplement or fortified
foods such as plant milks, nutritional yeast, and cereals. B12 is made by
bacteria, not by plants or animals. Also pay attention to **vitamin D** (sunlight
or a supplement), **iodine** (iodised salt), and **omega-3s** (flax, chia, walnuts,
or an algae-based DHA supplement). Talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before
making major dietary changes.

### Myth: "You need cow's milk for strong bones."
- **Fact:** Calcium is a mineral that plants take up from the soil, and many plant
  foods are good sources.
- **Plant calcium sources:** calcium-fortified plant milks, calcium-set tofu, kale,
  bok choy, broccoli, sesame seeds/tahini, chia seeds, almonds, dried figs.
  (Spinach is high in calcium but poorly absorbed.)
- **Bone health also depends on** weight-bearing exercise, vitamin D, adequate
  protein, and magnesium.

### Myth: "You can't get enough protein without meat or dairy."
- **Fact:** Plants make all the amino acids. Of these, humans must get nine from
  food. Eating a variety of plant proteins across the day supplies all nine.
  There's no need to combine them at every meal.
- **Plant protein sources:** dals and lentils, chana/chickpeas, rajma, tofu, tempeh,
  edamame, soy chunks, seitan, hemp seeds, peanuts, quinoa.

### Plant-powered athletes
*These athletes don't prove a nutritional point, but they do show what's
possible.*
- **Dotsie Bausch:** USA Olympic cyclist, team pursuit silver medallist at London
  2012, aged 39, on a plant-based diet.
- **Scott Jurek:** ultramarathoner who won the Western States 100 seven years in a
  row (1999–2005) on a vegan diet.
- **Patrik Baboumian:** strongman and world-record holder (including the yoke walk).
- **Nimai Delgado:** IFBB Pro bodybuilder, raised vegetarian, who has never eaten
  meat and has been vegan since ~2015. 🔎
- **Kendrick Farris:** three-time US Olympic weightlifter, vegan since 2014, who set
  the US record total of 377 kg in the 94 kg class at the 2016 Olympic Trials.

## 6. Plant-Based Swaps for Everyday Foods

| Lacto-vegetarian staple | Plant-based alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Doodh (milk) | Fortified soy milk (or oat milk) | Soy milk: ~7–8 g protein per cup, no cholesterol, less saturated fat. Choose calcium + B12 fortified. |
| Paneer | Extra-firm or calcium-set tofu | High protein, no cholesterol, a good calcium source when calcium-set. Works in palak, matar and tikka dishes. |
| Ghee / makhan | Mustard, olive or other plant oils; vegan butter | Swaps saturated fat for unsaturated fats. |
| Dahi / raita | Soy yoghurt (with live cultures) | Best protein of the plant yoghurts; probiotics if labelled "live cultures". Coconut yoghurt is lower in protein. |
| Lassi / chai with milk | Soy or oat milk | Oat milk froths well in chai; soy gives more protein. |
| Honey | Date syrup, jaggery (gur), maple syrup | Plant-derived sweeteners. Still added sugar, so use in moderation. |
